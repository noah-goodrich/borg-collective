# Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges

- Citation: Aman Singh Thakur, Kartik Choudhary, Venkat Srinik Ramayapally, Sankaran Vaidyanathan, Dieuwke Hupkes, "Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges", Proceedings of the Fourth Workshop on Generation Evaluation and Metrics (GEM2 2025), ACL, pp. 404-430; arXiv:2406.12624 (v6, 2025-08-18).
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12624
- Date accessed: 2026-10-30

- Access status: live (abstract only)

## Verified Quote(s)

Abstract:

> we find that only the best (and largest) models achieve reasonable alignment with humans.

> their assigned scores may still differ with up to 5 points from human-assigned scores.

> sensitivity to prompt complexity and length, and a tendency toward leniency.

> showing that judges with high percent agreement can still assign vastly different scores.

## Key finding

Across 13 judge models, only the strongest judges aligned reasonably with humans, and judges were lenient and sensitive to prompt length and complexity. High percent agreement can coexist with large score disagreements, so judge validation needs chance-corrected or class-wise metrics (for example Cohen's kappa, TPR/TNR) rather than raw percent agreement.
