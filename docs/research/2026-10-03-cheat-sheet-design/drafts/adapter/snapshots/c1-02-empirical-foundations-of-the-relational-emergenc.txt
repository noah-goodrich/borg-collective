This work presents the fully integrated empirical foundations of the Relational Emergence Framework, establishing it as a unified and falsifiable scientific meta-theory. The document consolidates the framework’s Laws and Axioms with explicit empirical principles, measurable signatures, and cross-domain validation spanning physics, biology, cognition, machine learning, and climate systems. The report introduces a key testable prediction: that integrated external tools reduce cognitive load and stabilize dual-task performance, demonstrating relational extension and load redistribution in hybrid cognitive systems. A comprehensive empirical mapping links each framework component to established scientific domains such as non-equilibrium thermodynamics, neural plasticity, pharmacodynamics, ecological stability, and distributed cognition. Each mapping includes specific, quantifiable outcomes—for example, convective roll formation (Nu), dendritic spine modulation, membrane potential stability, P300 amplitude reduction, binding affinity (Kd), and pupil-dilation-based cognitive load (CL). This unified empirical structure ensures replicability, operational clarity, and multiple avenues for falsification. By grounding abstract relational principles in measurable phenomena, the framework provides a rigorous cross-disciplinary lens for analyzing the emergence, persistence, and transformation of complex systems. The result is a complete, optimized empirical basis suitable for scientific critique, expansion, and further experimental testing.
