The rapid adoption of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in higher education has sparked debate over its impact on student learning and cognitive development. While initial institutional responses focused on defensive regulatory frameworks (McDonald et al., 2025), a more optimistic narrative has emerged from students, who report that GenAI enhances learning by supporting brainstorming and analytical reasoning (Crompton & Burke, 2023). However, a critical tension exists between these positive narratives and emerging insights from cognitive science, which warn of cognitive offloading (Risko & Gilbert, 2016), the tendency to delegate mental processing to GenAI tools, potentially leading to skill atrophy (Macnamara et al., 2024). Furthermore, empirical evidence validating these perceived gains remains limited. It remains unclear whether students reporting such improvements are engaging in the analytical reasoning and evaluative judgement that characterize critical-thinking development, or whether they are experiencing an illusion of competence, that is, mistaking the ease of generating articulate text for genuine learning.
