Cognitive Offloading is the use of physical action to reduce cognitive demand (Risko & Gilbert, 2016). Recently, it has been demonstrated that errors induce a tendency to switch to a different task (Spitzer et al., 2022). In Cognitive Offloading this effect seems to depend on the strategy that was used in the previous trial in that mental errors lead to an increased tendency to offload cognition in the next trial while offloading errors do not affect switch tendency (Aust et al., 2025). We suggest that this is due to capacity limited post-error processing. Since mental task completion requires more cognitive ressources than offloading parts of the task to an external device, errors - needing cognitive ressources as well - might lead to a tendency to offload cognition (thus post-error offloading). Here we test this idea by varying inter trial intervals (ITI). Shorter ITIs should lead to a greater post-error offloading effect. Longer ITIs should reduce this post-error offloading effect because sufficient parts of error processing has already taken place.
