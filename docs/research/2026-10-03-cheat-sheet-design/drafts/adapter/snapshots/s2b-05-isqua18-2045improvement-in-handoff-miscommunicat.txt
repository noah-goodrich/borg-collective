Objectives: The omission of critical information and the transfer of erroneous data during handoffs are too common. Such failures of communication contributes to two of every three “sentinel” events. Implementation of a handoff program was associated with 23% reductions in medical errors and 30% reductions in preventable adverse events without a negative effect on workflow (1). Our objectives were to determine whether introduction of a multifaceted handoff program was associated with improvements in communication, fewer omissions of key data in written handoffs, improved verbal handoffs and changes in physician workflow as well as reduction of interruptions during handoffs Methods: We conducted an uncontrolled before after IRB approved study on one general inpatient pediatric unit in faith based comunity hospital in Buenos Aires, Argentina introducing to staff the I-PASS program: the use of mnemonics to standardize handoff, the restructuring of verbal handoffs by minimizing interruptions and the use of written or computerized tools. Traning workshops, visual reminders and feedback on direct observation data and institutional commitment were used as some tof the components of the intervention. During the project we assessed the association of this intervention using univariate statistics with changes in outcomes such as incomplete handoff (using an IPASS evaluation tool by direct observation), rate of interruptions (counted during hahdoffs) and physician workflow (lengh of handoff).
