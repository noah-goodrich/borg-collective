## Key Findings

- Of 12 structured handoff tools searched, only SBAR (low certainty) and I-PASS (moderate certainty) had evidence of improved patient safety; no other tool was assessed in more than one study or setting.
- Most evidence is pre-post, raising risk of bias; for I-PASS only two randomized trials existed and neither found a statistically significant effect on patient clinical outcomes.
- Evidence is nearly all from academic training programmes; SBAR evidence is mostly nursing and I-PASS evidence is exclusively physicians, so cross-generalization is not established.
