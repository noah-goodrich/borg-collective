## Key Findings

- The picture-superiority effect disappeared when plain black-and-white pictures were compared with typographically distinctive
  words (varied font, size, colour, capitalisation).
- This is consistent with a physical-distinctiveness account and inconsistent with dual coding as the mechanism, in these tasks.
- Design implication (inference): a visual aid's benefit may come from making items distinctive, not from "adding a picture" as such.
