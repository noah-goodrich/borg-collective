## Key Findings

- Element selection was driven by what observers found MISSING from real handoffs (illness severity, contingency planning, receiver read-back), not by what is conventionally listed.
- A prior mnemonic (SIGNOUT) was reinforced with observation and feedback, yet a majority of verbal handoffs still did not follow it; residents asked for a shorter, easier-to-remember mnemonic with discrete elements and no overlap, integrated into the computerized handoff tool.
- SBAR is described as best for brief situational briefings with fewer than 5 key points and limited for complex patients who need broader information and context - the case that resembles a project hand-off.
- The receiver's synthesis (read-back) is a built-in element, making the format two-way rather than a one-way report.
