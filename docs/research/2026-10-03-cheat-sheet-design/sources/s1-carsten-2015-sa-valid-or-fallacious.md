## Key Findings

- Critics (Dekker and Hollnagel 2004) called SA a "folk model": common-sense but circular - error is explained by lack of SA and lack of SA is inferred from error.
- Sarter and Woods argued it is futile to fix "the" contents of SA in advance because the significance of any datum depends on context - a direct challenge to a fixed-content status display.
- Dekker links the concept to a "blame-the-individual" culture; Endsley rebuts that SA is a valid, useful, theory-backed construct that diagnoses system design.
- Middle positions: keep and augment the concept (Millot) or shift to distributed/system-level SA (Salmon, Walker, Stanton).
