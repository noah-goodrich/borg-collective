## Key Findings

- Debriefs ("after-action reviews") improve effectiveness over a control by about 25% (d = .67) across 46 samples.
- Effects were similar for teams and individuals, simulated and real settings, and medical and non-medical samples.
- Facilitated debriefs were roughly 3x as effective as non-facilitated ones (d = .75 vs .25), but very few studies had
  no facilitator, so the moderator claim is soft.
- The evidence is largely quasi-experimental; the authors say causal inference needs care and that alignment between
  participants, focus and level of measurement matters.
