## Key Findings

- The widely quoted Standish Chaos reports (16 percent success in 1994) are used to argue that PM best practices
  'hardly help'; Eveleens and Verhoef show the Standish success/challenged definitions are 'misleading, one-sided,
  pervert the estimation practice, and result in meaningless figures'.
- They tested the definitions on 5,457 forecasts of 1,211 real projects (hundreds of millions of euros) and the
  Standish figures did not match reality.
- Implication for RQ1: the on-time/on-budget/in-scope triple constraint that anchors PMBOK/PRINCE2 'success' is itself
  a shaky outcome measure, which is consistent with PMBOK 8 and PRINCE2 7 widening 'value' and adding sustainability.
- Caveat: it criticises a metric, not any framework; it provides no evidence for or against a particular
  decomposition.
