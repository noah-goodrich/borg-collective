## Key Findings

- A facilitated Reinertsen-style exercise on a $16M-lifetime-profit project produced one-month-delay cost estimates from
0 to $16M, with most between $150K and $2M.
- Even excluding the zero answer, the spread was about 1-to-100, consistent with Reinertsen's claimed ~50-to-1.
- Differences were traced to hidden assumptions (whether delay shifts or shrinks the profit curve), so surfacing
assumptions is the main benefit.
- It is a single informal replication; it does not establish that CoD-based prioritization improves outcomes.
