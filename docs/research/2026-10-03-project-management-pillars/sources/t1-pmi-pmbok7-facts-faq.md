## Key Findings

- PMI states the Seventh Edition moves 'from processes to principles' (12 principles) and 'from Knowledge Areas to
  project performance domains' (8 domains), a deliberate rejection of the process-group structure of editions 1-6.
- The principles 'summarize the what and why of project management' and are explicitly not agile-specific; approach
  (predictive, adaptive, hybrid) is a tailoring choice.
- A new 'tailoring' section and a 'Models, Methods, and Artifacts' catalogue were added, with fuller tool content
  moved to the paid PMIstandards+ platform (so the detail is paywalled).
- This structure did not last: PMBOK 8 (Nov 2025) reverts to 6 principles, 7 domains and 40 processes (see PMA card),
  so 'the' PMBOK decomposition is a moving target.

## Verified Quote(s)

**Location reference:** Page 1 of 1, numbered facts 3 and 4 (PDF 'pmbok-guide-seven-facts.pdf'). Companion FAQ PDF
(pmbok-guide-public-faqs-29-march-2021.pdf, Q7) states: 'The pending Standard for Project Management presents 12
principles of project management, but they are not specifically agile principles.'

> A move from processes to principles: The 12 project management principles in the draft of The Standard for Project
> Management are built around a set of statements that guide the actions and behaviors of project management
> practitioners regardless of development approach. The principles summarize the what and why of project management.

> A move from Knowledge Areas to project performance domains: The PMBOK® Guide is organized around eight project
> performance domains defined as a group of related activities that are critical for the effective delivery of project
> outcomes.
