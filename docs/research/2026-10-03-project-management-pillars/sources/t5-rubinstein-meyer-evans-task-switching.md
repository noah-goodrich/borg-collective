## Key Findings

- Alternating between tasks incurs a measurable switching-time cost compared with repeating the same task.
- The cost grows with the complexity of the rule being switched to and shrinks when a visual cue says which task is next.
- The effects are additive, supporting a two-stage executive-control model (goal shifting, then rule activation).
- Switching familiar-to-unfamiliar is slower than the reverse.
- Important caveat: the abstract does NOT contain the widely repeated "40% of productive time lost" figure; that number
  is a popular-press gloss (via APA press coverage) and was not verified by this card.
