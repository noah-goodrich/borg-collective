## Key Findings

- Scrum decomposes into three empirical pillars (transparency, inspection, adaptation), five values, one team with
  three accountabilities (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developers), five events with the Sprint as container, and
  three artifacts, so its 'pillars' are a process-control structure, not knowledge areas.
- It is explicitly founded on 'empiricism and lean thinking', which links it to the Poppendieck/Lean lineage and to
  Kanban's flow theory.
- It is deliberately silent on classic PM concerns: a text count of the fetched page finds 0 occurrences of 'budget',
  0 of 'procurement' and 1 of 'cost' (in a sentence about limiting the risk of cost per Sprint), so cost, procurement
  and portfolio governance are outside its decomposition.
- It treats a Sprint as 'a short project' and the Product Goal as the long-term objective, i.e. a project is
  decomposed into time-boxed learning cycles rather than phases.
- It asserts non-modularity: using only parts of Scrum 'is not Scrum'. That conflicts with the tailoring emphasis of
  PMBOK (a principle) and PRINCE2 (a principle), and with Kanban's 2025 removal of its own immutability claim: a
  genuine framework disagreement.
