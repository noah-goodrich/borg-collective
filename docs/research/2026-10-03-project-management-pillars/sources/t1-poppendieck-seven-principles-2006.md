## Key Findings

- The 2006 chapter lists seven principles: Eliminate Waste, Build Quality In, Create Knowledge, Defer Commitment,
  Deliver Fast, Respect People, Optimize the Whole. The popular 2003 wording differs (amplify learning, decide as late
  as possible, empower the team, build integrity in, see the whole); the authors themselves say 'the wording of some
  principles has changed a bit'.
- An earlier 2002 paper by one author
  (https://sel.unsl.edu.ar/ApuntesMaes/Anteriores/MetodologiasAgiles/LeanThinking.pdf, fetched and read) gave only
  four 'basic principles': add nothing but value, center on the people who add value, flow value from demand, optimize
  across organizations. So even the Lean list moved from four to seven in four years: the count of 'pillars' is a
  framing choice.
- It insists that principles are 'underlying truths' while practices must be adapted to the setting, and warns that
  copying practices without understanding principles 'has a long history of mediocre results', a direct argument for
  tailoring that PMBOK and PRINCE2 also adopt.
- Lean's decomposition is about value flow, quality and people, not scope/cost/schedule; it is orthogonal to, not a
  rival of, PMBOK/PRINCE2, and underlies both Scrum ('empiricism and lean thinking') and Kanban.
