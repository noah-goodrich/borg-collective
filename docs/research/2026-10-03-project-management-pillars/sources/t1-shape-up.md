## Key Findings

- Shape Up decomposes work into three phases (shaping, betting, building) run in six-week cycles with a cool-down, and
  explicitly says it 'targets a specific risk: the risk of not shipping on time'.
- Its signature moves are inversions of mainstream PM: fixed time with variable scope (appetite, not estimates), no
  backlog, and a 'circuit breaker' with no default extension when a bet overruns.
- It gives full responsibility to a small integrated team and keeps senior 'shapers' on a parallel track, so
  planning/definition work is decoupled from execution.
- It treats 'getting stuck' and runaway projects, not requirements capture, as the central failure mode, and
  explicitly leaves 'building the wrong thing' to other books.
- It is designed for a small company with product autonomy; adoption outside that context is contested (see the
  t1-fnune-year-of-shape-up-2020 card and forum evidence).
