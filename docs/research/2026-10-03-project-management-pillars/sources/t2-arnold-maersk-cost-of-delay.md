## Key Findings

- Maersk Line (a $100M IT portfolio in the paper) applied dollar-denominated Cost of Delay and CD3 scheduling to its
requirements lists.
- Reported CoD was far from evenly distributed: the top 25% of requirements were worth about three orders of magnitude
more than the bottom 25%, fitting a Pareto power-law curve.
- Implication: coarse categorization (MoSCoW) misses a heavy tail, so prioritization effort is best spent finding the
few very valuable items.
- A single feature that waited 38 weeks in queues was reported as nearly $8 million of missed opportunity at roughly
$210,000 per week, an illustration of queue cost.
- Evidence is author-reported in one firm; no controlled comparison against opinion-based ordering.
