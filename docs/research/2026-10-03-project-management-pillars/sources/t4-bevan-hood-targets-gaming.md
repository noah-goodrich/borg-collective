## Key Findings

- Targets-and-sanctions regimes rest on assumptions that measurement problems and gaming do not matter; the paper
  tests those assumptions against English health-service evidence.
- The evidence includes documented successes, so the finding is "targets work AND get gamed", not "targets fail".
- Gaming spreads: the authors argue a "Gresham's law" of reactive gaming can push actors who initially do not game
  toward gaming (full-text passage, seen via a mirror at straty.com, not on the LSE page).
- When reported performance meets the target, controllers cannot distinguish genuine success from gaming, which is why
  a single target number is an unreliable read of the underlying outcome.
