## Key Findings

- Narrative visualization is framed as sitting on a spectrum between "author-driven narrative
  flow" and "reader-driven story discovery," and every design choice (genre, tactic, structure)
  trades off along that spectrum.
- Seven genres are identified: magazine style, annotated chart, partitioned poster, flow chart,
  comic strip, slide show, and film/video/animation — this genre vocabulary is still the field's
  common reference set.
- Design tactics split into two families: "visual narrative tactics" (how attention is visually
  directed — highlighting, close-ups) and "narrative structure tactics" (how the story unfolds
  over time and how much interactivity/ordering control the reader retains).
- The paper's own case corpus (58 examples) came from online journalism, blogs, and vis-research
  demos — a convenience sample, not a systematic literature search, which is a real methodological
  limit later work (e.g. Kosara & Mackinlay 2013) has built on.
- The IEEE VIS community later granted this paper a Test-of-Time Award, an independent,
  field-level endorsement of its lasting influence rather than a claim the authors make about
  themselves.
