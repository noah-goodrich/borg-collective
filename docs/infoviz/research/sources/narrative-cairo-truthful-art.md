## Key Findings

- Cairo's framework (as summarized across independent course pages and reviews, since the book's
  full text could not be fetched live) orders visualization values as truthful/accurate first,
  then functional, then beautiful/engaging — engagement is not supposed to be pursued at the
  expense of accuracy.
- A direct-from-author quote captures the reading-not-seeing thesis central to his narrative
  argument: visualizations require active interpretation by the reader, not passive viewing.
- Independent secondary reviews (UW-Madison CS765 course pages, eagereyes) are split: one calls
  the companion volume ("The Functional Art") essential reading for anyone in visualization or
  visualization research, while a named critic on the same course page argues Cairo's approach to
  statistical rigor is "very dangerous — especially for journalists" — a genuine, citable point of
  disagreement worth carrying into the live-debates list.
- Cairo is positioned by course adopters as a bridge between academic InfoVis theory and
  day-to-day newsroom/graphics practice, which is why he is assigned alongside Segel & Heer in at
  least one data-visualization course syllabus found during this research.
- This card is a explicit example of the "cached/partial" access-status case flagged in the
  research brief: the book itself was not fetched live in this session; what is quoted here is
  verified from a live author interview page, and broader framework claims are corroborated via
  independent course/review pages rather than the primary text.
