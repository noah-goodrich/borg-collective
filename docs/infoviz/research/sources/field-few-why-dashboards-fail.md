## Key Findings

- Few's central, unhedged claim: **"Most dashboards that are used in businesses today fail. At best they deliver
  only a fraction of the insight that is needed to monitor the business."** — stated as an opinion/observation from
  consulting practice, not backed by cited data in this piece.
- He attributes the failure primarily to **vendor incentives, not technology**: vendors "work hard to make their
  dashboards shimmy with sex appeal" (gauges, meters, traffic lights) that are "cute" but whose "appeal is only
  skin deep," selling flash rather than clarity.
- His prescribed fix is perceptual, not decorative: dashboard design should be "informed by a solid understanding
  of visual perception and human cognition," borrowing directly from academic information-visualization research
  rather than vendor defaults — a bridge claim connecting the practitioner and academic schools.
- The commercial-jet-cockpit analogy is his go-to model of a working real-time operational display: designed so a
  pilot can "see what's going on at a glance," which he holds up as the standard operational dashboards should
  meet but usually don't.
- This is a load-bearing "boots-on-the-ground" counterpoint to the more academic/rhetorical viz-skeptic voices
  (Cairo, Bateman) in this corpus — it grounds the "most viz fails readers" claim in operational BI/dashboard
  deployments specifically, the closest analog in this corpus to Noah's stated "complex operational information"
  target audience.
