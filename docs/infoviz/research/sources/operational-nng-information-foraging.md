## Key Findings

- Information scent is defined as "how promising a potential source of information
  appears to the user," borrowed from optimal-foraging-theory terminology applied to
  information seeking (Pirolli & Card).
- Users satisfice rather than optimize: they will not scroll or click extensively if the
  effort-to-benefit ratio declines, which is the cognitive mechanism behind why
  single-screen, at-a-glance layouts (per Few) actually work for users.
- On a page, scent is carried by "the title, images, and the information that is easily
  visible above the fold" — directly implicating dashboard layout/hierarchy choices.
- Well-designed interfaces reduce the cognitive work of scent-following through layout
  and formatting (bulleted lists, bold keywords, descriptive headlines, clear visual
  hierarchy) rather than requiring users to develop workarounds.
- Misaligned scent — content that looks unrelated to the user's task — causes users to
  abandon the page/view, a direct mechanistic explanation for dashboard "trust" failures
  described elsewhere in this subfield's contrarian literature.
