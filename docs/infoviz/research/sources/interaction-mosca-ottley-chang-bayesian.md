## Key Findings

- Across two crowdsourced experiments, adding interaction to a static Bayesian-reasoning
  visualization did not improve participants' task accuracy — and "in some cases, it can
  significantly detract from it."
- The underlying static visualization's design (not just the presence/absence of interaction)
  modulates performance — meaning a well-chosen static encoding can outperform a poorly-matched
  interactive one.
- Users' spatial ability moderates the effect: people with high vs. low spatial ability "respond
  differently to different interaction techniques and underlying base visualizations" — interaction
  benefit is not uniform across users.
- The authors explicitly frame the paper as addressing an evidence gap: "there have been few
  attempts to empirically demonstrate whether adding interaction to a static visualization improves
  its function beyond popular beliefs" — this is a direct, methodologically strong rebuttal to the
  unexamined assumption that interactivity is inherently beneficial.
