## Key Findings

- Ware frames information visualization as fundamentally about "external cognition" — using resources outside
  the mind (interactive visual representations) to boost the cognitive capabilities of the mind, substituting
  perceptual relation-detection for cognitive inferencing and reducing demands on working memory.
- The book organizes vision science into a design-applicable sequence: low-level luminance/contrast/color
  perception (Chapters 3-4), preattentive processing and "information that pops out" (Chapter 5), pattern and
  object perception (Chapters 6-7) — establishing that different perceptual mechanisms operate at different
  processing stages and require different design rules.
- Preattentive processing is presented as the mechanism by which "the visual image is broken into elements of
  color, form, and motion" in a massively parallel, sub-conscious stage before focused attention — the
  theoretical basis for why certain encodings (color, size, orientation) can be scanned near-instantly while
  others require serial, attention-demanding search.
- Ware's explicit thesis is that perception science, correctly applied, is generative: "Following
  perception-based rules, we can present data in such a way that the important and informative patterns stand
  out. If we disobey these rules, our data will be incomprehensible or misleading" — directly relevant to the
  program's ELI10-readability success bar.
- The foreword (Stuart Card) situates the book within a maturity arc for the field — exploratory design, then
  characterization/theory (where this book sits), then validation, then engineering codification — implying
  that as of publication the perception-to-design translation is principled but not yet fully empirically
  validated at the level of finished designs.

## Inclusion Decision

**Decision:** Core
**Rationale:** This is the canonical practitioner/academic bridge text for the subfield; RQ2 explicitly asks
for the "must-read" canon, and this book is cited as the most-thorough treatment of perception-for-design
across nearly every other source found in this research pass (including the search-result summaries of Ware's
own standing in the field).

**Redundancy check:** Not redundant — while Cleveland & McGill (1984) and Heer & Bostock (2010) supply the
primary-research backbone for one specific claim (channel-ranking accuracy), Ware's book is the only source in
this set that synthesizes the *full range* of perceptual mechanisms (preattentive processing, color/lightness,
pattern/object perception, working memory) into a coherent design curriculum — no other keeper source
substitutes for it.
