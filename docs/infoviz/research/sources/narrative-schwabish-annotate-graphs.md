## Key Findings

- Three concrete annotation techniques, ordered from simple to complex: (1) adding labels
  directly to data series instead of a disconnected legend, (2) writing "active titles" that state
  the chart's argument rather than merely describing its axes, and (3) adding contextual detail
  directly inside the graphic.
- Amanda Cox, editor of The New York Times' Upshot, is quoted calling the annotation layer "the
  most important thing we do" in Upshot production — an institutional practitioner endorsement
  from a leading data-journalism outlet.
- John Burn-Murdoch of the Financial Times independently makes a parallel claim that annotation is
  "where the journalism really comes into visual journalism," corroborating Cox's view from a
  separate newsroom.
- The core mechanism argued for annotation is reducing reader decoding effort — moving the
  interpretive work from the reader's head onto the page so attention goes to the substance, not
  legend-matching or axis-reading.
- The piece does not address failure modes (over-annotation, annotation as compensation for a
  poorly chosen chart type), which is a gap relative to a fully-hedged treatment and is worth
  weighing against Kosara-style critiques of technique overuse in a future deep-dive.
