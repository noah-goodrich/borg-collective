## Key Findings

- The Data Visualization Society's own onboarding guidance recommends starting with **foundational concepts**
  (grammar of graphics, user-centered design, pre-attentive attributes, Gestalt principles, decluttering, color
  theory, chart text, accessibility) before tool-specific skills.
- Explicit tool-learning advice: **"pick one tool and learn how to use it really well. That will give you a
  foundation to learn other tools"** — a sequencing principle (concepts before tools, one tool deeply before many)
  directly useful for a curriculum design.
- The institutionally-endorsed reading canon spans BOTH the academic and practitioner traditions in a single
  list: Munzner's *Visualization Analysis and Design* (academic) alongside Cairo's *The Functional Art*/*The
  Truthful Art*/*How Charts Lie* and Knaflic's *Storytelling with Data* (practitioner) — corroborating that these
  are genuinely cross-school canonical, not this researcher's artifact of framing.
- The society explicitly names **ethics/accuracy resources** as a distinct pillar of the field's canon (Cairo's
  *How Charts Lie*, Ben Jones's *Avoiding Data Pitfalls*, the Urban Institute's *Do No Harm Guide*), confirming
  that "does this visualization mislead its reader" is treated as a first-class subfield concern by the field's
  own professional body, not just individual critics.
- Community-based, non-institutional learning venues (Nightingale journal, DVS itself, informal peer-critique
  communities like MakeoverMonday-style groups) are positioned as co-equal to formal reading, reflecting the
  field's self-taught, practice-heavy culture as institutionally sanctioned pedagogy.
