## Key Findings

- Core 2020 thesis: dashboards fail because organizations "misuse them by expecting them
  to do everything for us" — simultaneously wanting storytelling, aesthetics, rapid
  glanceable lookup, and ad hoc self-service analysis from one artifact.
- Illustrative failure modes cited: dashboards ballooning to dozens of pages, "death by
  1,000 filters," users not trusting the numbers and re-exporting to Excel to redo the
  analysis themselves.
- Proposed 2020 alternative: code notebooks (as popularized by Project Jupyter) as a more
  honest medium for ad hoc analytical exploration, leaving dashboards to do only fast
  numeric lookup.
- 2023 self-revision: the real root cause was not the dashboard artifact but
  organizational — "the relationships, communication, processes, and people" — and that
  "trust is also about relationships, communication, and even feeling free to make
  mistakes," not just number accuracy.
- The piece's cultural impact (250,000+ views, multiple direct published rebuttals) is
  itself evidence that the tension it names — over-tasking a single artifact with
  incompatible jobs — is a live, contested debate in the field rather than a settled
  question.
